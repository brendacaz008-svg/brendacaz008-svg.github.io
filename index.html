I am an undergraduate student at University of California, RIverside, pursuing a degree in Neuroscience through the College of Humanities, Arts, and Social Sciences (CHASS). I am deeply passionate about understandiing the complexities of the human brain, particularly how neurological processes influence cognition, behavior, memory, and overall health. My academic interests lie in between neuroscience, medicine, biomedical engineering, and an emphasis on involvement in research that can translate to meaningful advancements in healthcare to better the lifes of people. My primary research interests are in neurodegenerative diseases, eating disorders, neurobiology, epilepsy, and neuromodulation. I am especially fascinated by how neural circuits influence behavior and cognition as well as how new emerging technologies, such as deep brain stimulation (DBS), may provide alternative treatment options for individuals with neurological and psychiatric conditions. I hope to explore how advances in neuroscience can contribute to more effective and accessible treatments. My previous academic research experience has strengthened my interest in scientific investigation. Through an independent research project, I examined the relationship between impostor feelings, perceived academic difficulty, and high school students' decisions to pursue STEM-related majors. This experience introduced me to research methodology, quantitative data collection, statistical analysis, and interpreting scientific findings. It also reinforced my interest in understanding how psychological and environmental factors influence individual experiences and opportunities. As I continue my undergraduate education, I am seeking opportunities to expand my research experience, develop laboratory skills, and collaborate with professionals in neuroscience and related disciplines. I am particularly interested in neurobiological research, clinical neuroscience, and emerging medical technologies. I value interdisciplinary learning and believe that meaningful scientific progress comes from integrating perspectives across biology, psychology, medicine, engineering, and public health. An important part of my long-term vision is addressing disparities in neurological healthcare, particularly those affecting historically underrepresented communities. I am interested in understanding how socioeconomic circumstances, cultural factors, and unequal access to specialized medical services influence neurological outcomes. I hope to contribute to research and healthcare initiatives that make scientific advancements more accessible to diverse populations, particularly Hispanic and Latino communities. Ultimately, I aspire to pursue a career in medicine and neuroscience, with a particular interest in neurology and neuromodulation. My goal is to combine clinical knowledge, scientific research, and technological innovation to contribute to advancements in neurological treatment. I approach my education with curiosity, determination, and a commitment to continuous growth. I believe that becoming an effective scientist and healthcare professional requires not only academic achievement but also critical thinking, collaboration, and an understanding of the individuals behind the research. I look forward to continuing to develop as a student, researcher, and future healthcare professional while contributing to the evolving field of neuroscience.
